# Carinya Parc — Brand Voice

## Who we are

**Name.** Carinya is an Aboriginal word meaning "peaceful home," carried into the paddocks of The Branch, Upper Hunter NSW. Everything we make should feel like arriving somewhere restful, generous and alive — a home the land is healing into.

**Positioning.** A working regenerative farm you're invited into — where healing degraded land, growing nutrient-dense food and welcoming people are one act of care.

**Mission.** To heal land and food systems through hands-on ecological restoration, nutrient-dense produce, and collective action.

**Founder.** Jonathan Daddia ("Jonno") — a strategic leader who traded the boardroom for a pair of boots and a plan. Brings evidence-based rigour to regenerative farming: measure everything, share it openly, let the land teach the rest. Voice is written in his first-person words.

## Personality

Warm & welcoming · Grounded & authentic · Rugged & hands-on · Quietly playful

## Values

1. **Regeneration over extraction.** Leave it healthier than we found it.
2. **Stewardship over ownership.** The land is borrowed from our grandkids — from those to come.
3. **Collaboration & transparency.** Many hands, open books. We publish wins and setbacks alike.

## Voice & tone

- **Person:** first person plural ("we", "our"); address the reader as "you."
- **Register:** warm, plain, first-person, in Jonno's words. Speak like someone talking across the fence, not presenting to a boardroom.
- **Tone:** grounded and hopeful. Never preachy, never greenwashed.
- **Evidence over slogans:** concrete over abstract. Say "42 hectares," "30,000 trees," "104 acres of habitat corridor" — not "a lot of land" or "huge impact."
- **Casing:** sentence case everywhere, except the wordmark and eyebrows, which are UPPERCASE and letterspaced.
- **Emoji:** none, ever.
- **Signature line:** "A peaceful home for land, food & community."

### Do

- Speak plainly, first person, and invite people in.
- Lead with what's actually happening on the ground — a season, a planting day, a number.
- Name the place: The Branch, Upper Hunter NSW, the river, the paddocks.
- Show the doing — hands, tools, weather, animals.
- Own setbacks alongside wins.

### Don't

- Greenwash, lecture, or hide behind jargon.
- Reach for abstract sustainability language when a concrete detail is available.
- Use emoji or exclamation-heavy hype.
- Speak as an institution — this is Jonno and the land, not a brand voice-over.

## Regulatory note (carries into claim policy, not just tone)

Regenerative and environmental claims made in copy (soil health, biodiversity, "sustainable," "regenerative") sit under live ACCC guidance on environmental and sustainability claims — this is a compliance surface, not only a style one. Per `docs/product.md`, no published claim should outrun its evidence, and the eventual `packages/brand/src/claim-policy.md` is the authority once built. Until then: don't assert what isn't measured or sourced; prefer the specific, checkable number over the sweeping claim.

## Sample lines (reference, not a script)

- "We treat the land as borrowed from our grandkids."
- "Come get your hands dirty — every tree counts, and there's a place for you here."
- "We didn't inherit healthy land. We're growing it back."
- "Many hands, open books."

## Vocabulary

- **The Branch** — the river / property name, always capitalised as a place name.
- **Carinya Parc** — always written in full on first reference per piece; not abbreviated to "Carinya" or "CP" in running prose (CP is reserved for the monogram mark).
- Prefer **"regenerating," "restoring," "healing"** the land over "developing" or "improving."
- Prefer **"herd"** for the Highland cattle, not "livestock" in narrative copy.
- **Upper Hunter, NSW** — used in bylines/eyebrows; "The Branch" for the specific location.

## Channels

Primary: website copy, the "Life on Pasture" blog/journal, email newsletter (progress reports, planting days, produce news). Secondary: Instagram, YouTube. Tone flexes slightly warmer/more informal on social, stays evidence-led on the blog and in email.
