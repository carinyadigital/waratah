# Editorial rubric

What pass 3 edits against, and what the human reviewer's `humanScore` (1–5) anchors to. Five dimensions, equal weight. A piece ships when nothing scores below 3 and the mean is ≥ 4.

## 1. Point

**5** — One clear point, stated early, earned by the end. A reader can repeat it a day later.
**3** — The point exists but the reader has to assemble it.
**1** — A topic, not a point.

## 2. Evidence

**5** — Every load-bearing claim is annotated, sourced, and the numbers carry units, baselines and dates. Setbacks appear where they exist.
**3** — Sourced, but numbers float free of baselines, or the setbacks are missing.
**1** — Adjectives doing the work of measurements.

## 3. Voice

**5** — Sounds like Jonno across the fence: plain, warm, dry, specific to this place. No banned terms, no hype, no hedge.
**3** — Clean but generic; could be any farm.
**1** — Corporate or greenwashed. The one-line positioning test fails.

## 4. Structure

**5** — Skimmable: honest headings, short paragraphs, one idea each; the surface spec's shape is met without feeling templated.
**3** — Sound but baggy; sections overlap or the ending trails off.
**1** — A wall, or listicle filler.

## 5. Usefulness

**5** — The reader can do something with it: a method, a measurement to copy, a recipe that works, a reason to visit.
**3** — Interesting but inert.
**1** — Content for content's sake — the thing this practice exists to never publish.
